Performance Marketing

Measurement-led acquisition focused on efficient growth, not vanity metrics. Performance marketing only works when every rupee is accountable — so I set up clean measurement first, then plan full-funnel paid media across Google, Meta and TikTok, with budgets, creative direction and reporting all managed against one number: your return on ad spend.

Why it matters

The problems this fixes

Most ad accounts fail the same way: spend goes up, learning doesn't. Here's what changes.

Spend with no accountability

The budget grows every month but nobody can say which campaign actually earned it. Reports celebrate impressions while the sales line stays flat.

Tracking you can't trust

Conversions are double-counted, key events are missing, and platform numbers disagree with your own records. Every decision is built on guesswork.

Campaigns on autopilot

An account set up once and "optimised" by quarterly tweaks. Creative fatigues, audiences saturate, and costs creep up while nobody notices.

Process

How a project runs

Measurement before spend. Testing before scaling. Always.

01

Audit & tracking

I review your accounts, analytics and funnels, then fix measurement first. If we can't measure it, we don't spend on it.

02

Strategy & budgets

You get a full-funnel plan — channels, audiences, budgets and targets — with success defined before a single ad goes live.

03

Launch & test

Campaigns launch with a structured testing plan. Early weeks are about learning cheaply: which audiences respond, which creative wins.

04

Scale & report

Winners get budget, losers get cut, and you get a monthly report tied to revenue — not impressions. The cycle repeats, sharper each time.

Fit

Is this service right for you?

Performance marketing rewards businesses ready to spend seriously and measure honestly. This fits when:

  • You're already spending — or ready to — on Google, Meta or TikTok, and want growth you can measure, not reports full of impressions.
  • Your product and funnel are proven — ads amplify what works; they're an expensive way to discover that nothing converts.
  • You want one person accountable — for tracking, creative direction, budgets and results, instead of three vendors pointing at each other.
